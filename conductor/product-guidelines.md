# Product Guidelines: KidShow Scout

## Brand Identity
### Tone & Voice
- **Playful & Friendly:** The voice of KidShow Scout should feel like a trusted companion in the world of kids' media. Use energetic, welcoming language that avoids jargon but maintains clarity.
- **Empathetic:** Acknowledge the care parents put into choosing content. We aren't just a database; we are a partner in safe discovery.

## Visual Design
### Core Aesthetics
- **Rounded & Soft Shapes:** To maintain a "safe" and friendly feel, all UI components (cards, buttons, modals) must use large border radii (e.g., `1.5rem` or higher). This mirrors the inviting style of top-tier children's brands.
- **Vibrant Palette:** Use a bold, energetic color scheme. Primary actions and status indicators should be high-contrast and easy to identify.

### Typography
- **Friendly & Approachable:** Use rounded sans-serif fonts (e.g., Fredoka, Quicksand) for all headings and UI elements. These fonts reinforce the playful brand identity and pair perfectly with the soft-edged visual style.

## Content & Safety
### Safety Communication
- **Direct & Transparent:** We prioritize honesty. When a show is flagged (Caution/Unsafe), the reasoning must be explicit (e.g., "Contains fast-paced editing," "Includes mature themes").
- **Categorical Clarity:** While providing detail, we also use clear, color-coded safety buckets (Safe = Green, Caution = Yellow, Unsafe = Red) to allow for instant decision-making.

### Age Representation
- **Human-Readable Precision:** While the backend uses a decimal system (e.g., `0.5`, `2.0`), the frontend must always format these as friendly labels (e.g., "5 Months", "2 Years") to ensure the data is accessible and intuitive for parents.

## User Experience
### Interaction Principles
- **Visual-First Discovery:** Show cards should be the focal point, using high-quality imagery and clear status badges.
- **Frictionless Filtering:** Controls for age and stimulation should be tactile and intuitive, requiring minimal cognitive load for busy parents.
